**Overall view.** CIMB grows profit steadily but slowly, and more of that profit now comes from Malaysia. FY2025 profit was a record RM7.85b, but it rose only 1.7%. Interest margins narrowed in every market as rates were cut across ASEAN, and a stronger ringgit reduced the value of profits from Indonesia and Thailand. Two things made up for this: cost discipline and non-interest income. **Treasury client sales were the fastest-growing part of the franchise, up 7.2% y-o-y.** In 1H FY26 the income mix improved. Non-interest income rose two quarters in a row, the cost-to-income ratio fell to 45.2%, and the impaired loans ratio reached a record low. That supports a steady ROE of about 11% and a dividend payout of about 55%. The main weak point is the interest margin. CIMB's margin is structurally thinner than Maybank's, so it has less room to absorb competition for deposits.

## 2. Income analysis

### 2.1 Revenue mix (FY2025)

```
Operating income RM22.5b
├── Net interest income (incl. Islamic)  ~RM15.4b (~68%)  ← loans, deposits, Islamic financing
└── Non-interest income                   RM7.1b  (~32%)  ← fees, treasury/markets, IB, other
```

### 2.2 Net interest income
- **FY2025:** net interest income was **flat** in ringgit terms but **up about 3.2% in constant currency**. The ringgit strengthened against the IDR and THB, which lowered the value of Indonesian and Thai earnings when converted. NIM **fell 8bp**, the result of OPR and regional rate cuts. By 4Q25 the margin had started to stabilise (+2bp q-o-q to 2.10%).
- **Funding advantage:** the **CASA ratio is 42.7%**, one of the highest among Malaysian banks. Management leaned on cheap deposits to cushion the pressure on asset yields.
- **1H FY26:** in 1Q, net interest income dipped to RM3.7b as NIM slipped 2bp. Country-level margins still improved q-o-q: Malaysia +1bp, Singapore +12bp, Thailand +5bp. In 2Q, net interest income recovered 1.3% q-o-q to RM3.73b. Loans grew 1.1% q-o-q and deposits 2.5% (constant currency).

### 2.3 Non-interest income
- **FY2025: +3.1% to RM7.1b.** Treasury client sales rose **7.2%** and fees and commissions **3.2%**.
- **1Q FY26:** NOII **+11.9% q-o-q to about RM1.7b** because of stronger trading and FX gains. This was the opposite of Maybank, where lower market gains pulled 1Q down.
- **2Q FY26:** NOII **+6.0% q-o-q to RM1.83b** from higher franchise fees and continued growth in client sales.
- Touch 'n Go (TNG Digital), in which CIMB holds a stake, more than doubled its profit in 1H26. That is becoming a real associate contribution.

### 2.4 Costs, asset quality, capital
- CIR improved **200bp q-o-q to 45.2%** in 2Q26 as opex fell 1.6%. This is well inside the FY25 guidance of below 46.7%, and CIMB's cost efficiency is better than Maybank's (about 49%).
- **GIL ratio was 1.6% in Jun-26, a record low.** FY25 credit-cost guidance was 25–35bp.
- **CET1 fell from 14.3% (Dec-25) to 14.0% (Jun-26)**, which is still a comfortable buffer. It is about 90bp below Maybank.

### 2.5 Geographic mix
| Market | Datapoint |
|---|---|
| Malaysia | PBT share **rose from 57% to 61%** in FY25 as capital was reallocated to the more stable home market |
| Indonesia (CIMB Niaga) | FY25 PBT **IDR8.8tn (RM2.04b)**, +1.15%; record CASA growth |
| Thailand (CIMB Thai) | 1H26 net profit **RM181m**; 2Q profit up more than 5x on lower credit losses |
| Singapore | 12bp q-o-q NIM expansion in 1Q26 |

## 3. Treasury: where the revenue comes from

CIMB's treasury business is **Treasury & Markets**, part of **Group Wholesale Banking** (with Corporate Banking and Investment Banking). It covers FX, interest rates, credit and fixed income, money markets, equity derivatives and commodities. It has onshore desks in Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia and Thailand.

| # | Revenue source | What drives it | How it went in FY25–1H26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Treasury client sales**: FX, derivatives and hedging for corporates, commercial/SME and wealth clients | Trade flows, hedging demand, cross-selling to loan clients | **Fastest-growing part of the franchise, +7.2% y-o-y in FY25.** Momentum continued into 2Q26. |
| 2 | **FX trading and positioning** | Moves in MYR, IDR, THB and SGD | Mixed. It was a headwind on translation in FY25, then a **tailwind in 1Q26** (NOII +11.9% q-o-q on trading and FX). |
| 3 | **Fixed income trading and market-making** (MGS, GII, corporate bonds and sukuk, and IDR/THB/SGD government bonds) | Rate and yield-curve moves, principal-dealer flows | Supported by rate cuts in 2025 and 1H26 (bond prices rise when rates fall) |
| 4 | **Investment portfolio (FVOCI) and balance-sheet management gains** | Rate cycle, liquidity portfolio | Opportunistic gains as rates fell *(analyst inference)* |
| 5 | **Structured products and derivatives** (IRS, CCS, options, commodity hedges, structured investments) | Corporate risk management and wealth distribution | Contributes to client sales and wealth fees |

Treasury also draws on CIMB's **debt capital markets (DCM)** business. CIMB Investment Bank is the leading ringgit bond house:
- **#1 in ringgit bonds and sukuk in 2025** by Dealogic, with a **32.5%** market share for bonds and **15.3%** for sukuk, and **#1 in global sukuk among ASEAN banks** (Bloomberg).
- Named **Euromoney Asia-Pacific's Best Sukuk House 2026**, and CIMB Islamic was Asia's Best Sukuk House 2025.
- It is the **#1 Bank Negara Malaysia Principal Dealer** for both conventional and Islamic papers.

The two businesses feed each other. DCM originates bonds and sukuk, Treasury & Markets underwrites, distributes and makes markets in them, and the bank earns spread and flow income on the secondary trading.

### Products and instruments they focus on
1. **Ringgit government and corporate bonds and sukuk**, including MGS, GII and AAA/AA corporate bonds and sukuk. CIMB is the #1 principal dealer and #1 DCM house, and these instruments make up most of its liquid-asset portfolio.
2. **Multi-currency ASEAN FX.** CIMB makes markets in MYR, IDR, THB and SGD. Its onshore presence in the four largest ASEAN markets is a clear differentiator.
3. **Hedging solutions**: interest rate swaps, cross-currency swaps and FX options for corporates and commercial clients. These are cross-sold with loans under the Forward30 strategy. This is the main source of "treasury client sales".
4. **Islamic treasury**: shariah-compliant hedging and sukuk. CIMB is one of the world's top sukuk houses.
5. **Local-currency bonds across ASEAN** (IDR, THB, SGD) for regional institutional investors.
6. **Structured products and commodities** for corporate risk management and for wealth/private-banking clients.

### What this means for the treasury view
- **CIMB's treasury is more franchise-driven and more regional than Maybank's.** Growth comes from **client sales** of FX, hedging and bonds, which are recurring, rather than from proprietary positions. That explains why CIMB's NOII rose in 1Q26 while Maybank's markets income fell.
- **Sensitivity to interest rates:** more rate cuts would help bond trading and FVOCI gains, but NIM, already the thinnest among the large banks, would come under more pressure. The balance between the two is less favourable than at Maybank because CIMB starts from a lower margin.
- **FX sensitivity:** a stronger ringgit reduces the translated value of profits from CIMB Niaga and CIMB Thai (the 1Q26 "FX headwinds"). It can also push up client hedging volumes.

| | CIMB | Maybank |
|---|---|---|

| FY25 ROE | 11.3% | 11.7% |
| 1H26 ROE | 11.2% (2Q) | 12.0% |

| CASA ratio | 42.7% | 40.5% |
| Latest CIR | 45.2% | 49.1% |
| Latest CET1 | 14.0% | 14.92% |
| Dividend payout | ~55% | ~72.5% |
| Treasury trend 1Q26 | **NOII +11.9% q-o-q** (trading and FX) | Markets income fell sharply |

In short, CIMB runs at lower cost and has a stronger treasury franchise. Maybank earns a higher ROE and pays a larger share of profit as dividends.

## 5. Risks and catalysts

**Upside**
- Forward30 targets ROE improvement and cross-selling.
- Treasury client sales and wealth fees keep growing.
- CIMB Thai is recovering, and TNG Digital is growing strongly.
- Capital is being reallocated to Malaysia.
- Cost efficiency may improve further.

**Downside**
- NIM is thin and deposit competition for CASA is heating up.
- A stronger ringgit reduces the value of IDR and THB earnings.
- CIMB Niaga is growing slowly.
- CET1 has been drifting down.
- The payout ratio is lower than Maybank's, which makes CIMB less attractive to yield-focused investors.
- Credit costs are unusually low and could normalise upward.

---

## 6. Conclusion

Of the large Malaysian banks, CIMB has the most regional mix and the most franchise-driven treasury business. It leads the ringgit bond and sukuk market and is the #1 BNM principal dealer. Its treasury revenue now comes mainly from **client flow**: FX, rate hedging, bonds and sukuk, and structured products sold to corporates, SMEs and wealth clients. That income is more recurring, and it held up better than Maybank's through 1H26. The main question for the investment case is the interest margin. If NIM holds around 2.1% while non-interest income stays above 30% of revenue and CIR below 46%, ROE should rise steadily toward the Forward30 targets.
