**Overall view.** Maybank is a stable, high-payout ASEAN bank that grows slowly. In FY2025 it made a record RM10.5b profit, but growth was only low single digits. Three things drove it: a steady net interest margin, a strong fall in impairment charges, and cost control. Treasury income did not help. It was the weak spot in FY2025 and fell further in 1H FY26. Growth in "core" fees from wealth, bancassurance and investment banking made up for it. The FY26 ROE target of at least 11.8% looks achievable, since 1H FY26 ROE was 12.0%. That result depends more on low provisions and cost discipline than on revenue growth.

## 2. Income analysis

### 2.1 Revenue mix (FY2025)

```
Net operating income RM30.38b
├── Net fund-based income   RM20.23b  (~67%)   ← loans, deposits, Islamic financing
└── Non-interest income     RM10.15b  (~33%)   ← fees, treasury/markets, insurance, other
```

About two-thirds of revenue is spread income, so margins and funding costs matter most for earnings. A one-third share of non-interest income is high for a Malaysian bank. It comes from Etiqa (insurance and takaful), Maybank Investment Bank, and a large Global Markets (treasury) franchise. That diversification helps. The downside is that part of the non-interest income depends on the market cycle.

### 2.2 Net fund-based income
- **FY2025: +2.7% to RM20.23b.** Loan expansion drove the increase. NIM held at **2.05%** even though Bank Negara cut the OPR and other regional central banks eased. 4Q FY25 NIM rose 7bp q-o-q to **2.09%**.
- **Deposit franchise:** CASA grew 9.4% and the CASA ratio reached **40.5%** (41.1% by Mar-26). This is what kept margins intact. Cheaper deposits offset lower yields on assets.
- **Loans:** group loans grew only **+1.7%** in FY2025, although Malaysia grew **+6.1%** and Singapore **+5.0%**. The gap is most likely a translation effect: the ringgit strengthened in 2025, which lowers the ringgit value of foreign-currency loans. A weaker Indonesian loan book may also have contributed. *(analyst inference)*
- **1H FY26:** 1Q NFBI rose +3.2% to RM5.11b and NIM reached 2.14%. In 2Q, NFBI was flat (+0.1% to RM5.39b) because higher funding costs in Malaysia and Indonesia narrowed the margin. Loans picked up (+1.7% q-o-q, +2.7% y-o-y, to RM695.9b).

### 2.3 Non-interest income
- **FY2025: +2.7% to RM10.15b.** The components moved in different directions:
  - ↑ **Wealth income**, including bancassurance commissions and investment product sales
  - ↑ **Loan- and trade-related fees**
  - ↑ **Gains from selling securities at Maybank Singapore**
  - ↓ **Treasury income**, because FX gains and trading-securities income were lower
- **Insurance and takaful (Etiqa), 9M FY25:** underwriting income rose **+23.7% to RM840.6m**. PBT still fell to **RM941m** from RM1.01b because softer equity markets and yield-curve movements reduced investment income.
- **1H FY26:** NoII was about **RM4.1b** *(derived: 1Q RM1.99b + 2Q ~RM2.12b)*. Annualised, that is well below FY2025's RM10.15b. Market-related income was the main reason 1Q FY26 profit fell (RM2.48b vs RM2.59b). Core fees partly offset this in 2Q. Wealth management fees rose **+61.5%**, investment banking fees **+58.4%**, and banking-related fees **+2.6%**.

### 2.4 Costs and provisions
- 2Q FY26 overheads fell **2.5% y-o-y to RM3.69b**, and the cost-to-income ratio was **49.1%**.
- Net impairment charges were "significantly lower". This was the main reason 2Q profit rose even though fund-based income was flat.
- Asset quality is stable. GIL moved from 1.28% to 1.34%, and loan-loss coverage stays above 100%. The one watch point is that coverage has slipped from 106.7% to 104.4%.

### 2.5 Segment profit (latest available)
| Segment | Period | PBT | y-o-y |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group Community Financial Services (retail, SME, business banking) | FY2025 | RM5.28b | -7.3% |
| Group Global Banking (corporate, **Global Markets/treasury**, IB, transaction banking) | 9M FY25 | RM6.21b | +18.3% |
| Group Insurance & Takaful (Etiqa) | 9M FY25 | RM0.94b | -6.8% |

Group Global Banking is now the biggest profit engine, and Global Markets sits inside it. Community Financial Services earned less because retail and SME margins were squeezed and provisions were normalising.

## 3. Treasury (Global Markets): where the revenue comes from

Maybank's treasury activities sit mostly in **Group Global Banking → Global Markets**. Balance-sheet management sits in Group Treasury / ALM. Treasury revenue has five main sources:

| # | Revenue source | What drives it | How it went in FY25–1H26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Client FX sales** (spot, forwards, swaps for corporates, SMEs and wealth clients) | Trade flows, hedging demand, FX volatility | **Growing.** GM FX sales income +7.1% in 1Q FY25. FX volumes +2.9% (wealth) and +8.7% (SME) in 9M FY25. "Stronger global markets sales" in 1Q FY26. |
| 2 | **FX trading and revaluation gains** (proprietary and positioning) | USD/MYR, USD/IDR and USD/SGD moves | **Main drag.** "Lower FX gains" in FY25. 1Q FY26 hit by "sharply lower markets gain". |
| 3 | **Fixed income trading** (MGS, GII, corporate bonds and sukuk in the trading book, FVTPL) | Rate and yield-curve moves, market-making | **Lower.** "Lower trading securities income" in FY25. |
| 4 | **Investment portfolio gains** (sale of FVOCI securities: government and quasi-government bonds, sukuk) | Taking gains when rates fall | **Supportive.** Maybank Singapore booked gains on securities sales in FY25. |
| 5 | **Rates, derivatives and structured products** (IRS, cross-currency swaps, options, structured investments/deposits sold through wealth) | Corporate hedging, wealth-client demand | Growing alongside the wealth franchise (wealth fees +61.5% in 2Q FY26) |

Maybank IB's **debt capital markets** business works closely with Global Markets. It originates bonds and sukuk that Global Markets then distributes and makes markets in. In 2025 Maybank IB was **#1 in the RAM League Table for corporate bonds and sukuk**. It arranged **RM19.22b**, close to one-third of the market. Maybank Islamic is also a top-two ringgit sukuk house on Bloomberg's league table.

### Products and instruments they focus on
1. **Ringgit rates and sukuk.** This covers MGS/GII, government-guaranteed paper and AAA/AA corporate bonds and sukuk. Maybank is the leading local market-maker and underwriter, and these instruments also make up most of its liquidity (HQLA) portfolio.
2. **FX, especially client flow.** The focus is USD/MYR, SGD and IDR, served through the three home markets (Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia). Management is moving the mix toward **client-driven FX sales**, which are recurring, and away from positioning, which is volatile. FY25–1H26 results show why: client sales grew while trading gains fell.
3. **Islamic treasury.** Maybank Islamic is the largest Islamic bank in ASEAN. It offers shariah-compliant hedging (Islamic profit-rate swaps, Islamic FX forwards), commodity murabahah deposits and sukuk distribution.
4. **Hedging solutions for corporates and SMEs**, mainly interest rate swaps and cross-currency swaps. These are cross-sold with loans and trade finance, in line with the "loan- and trade-related fees" growth.
5. **Wealth-linked structured products and bonds** for Premier and Private clients. This area had the fastest-growing fee line in 1H FY26.

### What this means for the treasury view
- **Quality of treasury income is improving, but the amount is falling.** Recurring client-flow income is growing. Trading and FX revaluation income has fallen and was the single largest reason 1Q FY26 profit dropped.
- **Sensitivity to interest rates:** if OPR and regional rates fall further, trading and FVOCI gains would benefit (bond prices rise) but NIM would come under pressure. The two partly offset each other. If rates rise, the effects reverse.
- **FX sensitivity:** a stronger ringgit reduces the translated value of the Singapore and Indonesia books and has weighed on FX revaluation. The MIDF note on 1Q FY26, titled "currency headaches", makes the same point.

## 4. Risks and catalysts

**Upside**
- The ROAR30 strategy (announced Jan 2026) aims to raise ROE and fee income.
- Wealth and investment-banking momentum may continue.
- Malaysian loan growth of about 6% is outpacing the industry.
- The high dividend payout (70–75%) gives a yield floor.

**Downside**
- NIM could compress because funding costs are rising in Malaysia and Indonesia (seen in 2Q FY26).
- Treasury and markets income could stay volatile.
- Loan growth in Indonesia (Maybank Indonesia) is weak.
- Credit costs are unusually low and could normalise upward.
- A stronger ringgit reduces the translated value of overseas earnings.
- Loan-loss coverage is slowly declining.

---

## 5. Conclusion

Maybank's earnings are defensive and dividend-driven. Deposits (CASA above 40%) protect NIM, capital is strong (CET1 around 15%), and credit costs are low. **Treasury is still a large contributor but has become the most volatile line.** Its FX and trading gains fell in FY2025 and again in 1Q FY26. At the same time, its client-flow franchise (FX sales, ringgit bonds and sukuk, and hedging) keeps growing. Where earnings go next depends on three things: whether core fees keep growing, whether funding costs can be contained to hold NIM around 2.1%, and whether markets income stabilises from 2H FY26.
